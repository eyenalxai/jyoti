Choose the color algorithm

Three candidates, eight real captured scenes, 1.5 Hz. The question is not which algorithm is cleverest. It is which one makes a dark room feel like the screen without turning a white document blue.

Recommendation

Chroma-weighted OKLab dominant, warm-started, with a neutral gate

The research default survives the prototype. k-means on the 128 × 72 OKLab grid, k = 5, score population^0.8 × (0.2 + 0.8 × min(C / 0.15, 1)), drop clusters under 3% population, keep the winner's hue. Start each tick from the previous tick's centroids: it removes the single-tick cluster flips without slowing the hard cuts.

The derived color is emitted at a fixed OKLab lightness of 0.65, with chroma capped at 0.13 (the research cap at full brightness). The scene's mean OKLab lightness is published separately as a brightness signal; the UX ticket owns the mapping to bulb brightness. A neutral scene emits the temperature-mode fallback at 4000 K, not an RGB white.

Neutral gatep90 OKLCH chroma < 0.03, with hysteresis to 0.045 before leaving neutral.
DeadbandΔEOK 0.02 against the last acknowledged color, inherited from JYO-21.
Smoothing450 ms OKLab EMA for color, 900 ms for the brightness signal.

What failed

Saliency background is out. On the slow-drift scene it locks onto the static grey bar and reports chroma 0.00 for the whole 30 seconds while the coloured field sweeps the hue wheel; on the movie it averages the dark background and reports a mean hue error of 41°. It is stable and cheap, and it is exactly the “just picks the background” failure the research warned about.

Hue histogram is viable but second. It tracks the slow drift as well as dominant and is faster (0.1 ms vs 0.3–1.3 ms), but its 10° bins raise the movie's p90 hue error from 13° to 21°, and it can sit on one bin through a dim scene. Good fallback if k-means ever needs replacing; not the default.

Cold-started k-means flips. Without warm start, the dominant algorithm produces single-tick hue jumps on the dim scene (15 hue flips) and occasional flips on the fast-drift pattern. Warm start drops the dim scene to zero at no extra cost, and both variants follow every hard cut in the steps scene within one tick.
